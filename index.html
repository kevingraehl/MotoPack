    How to check photo accuracy

    For a ⅛-inch fit target, check a second measured span against the scaled image to within ⅛ inch (3.2 mm). A single side photo cannot measure depth or correct perspective.

    How to get a good frame photo

    In your garage or at a dealership, bring a tape measure and use the 1× camera. Stand about 5–10 feet back and lower the lens to the middle of the battery and motor opening. Aim square to the side. Place a ruler in the same plane as the opening.

    On eBay, search the model and year plus “bare frame.” Ask the seller for a full-resolution side photo and the center-to-center distance between two marked holes A and B. Ask for a second measurement to check scale, plus the narrowest usable width across the frame.

    Explore anti-squat, chain pull and swingarm travel ↗

    Motor placement and chain tension

    Start with the OEM countershaft center relative to the swingarm pivot. Anti-squat describes how drive forces act on the suspension. Sprocket position also changes how chain tension varies through travel.

    Check the tightest point in suspension travel. As the swingarm moves, the distance between the sprockets changes. The chain can go tight mid-stroke even when it has slack at rest. Excess tension can overload the output shaft and bearings and can crack the motor case.

    On the actual bike, check slack through the full stroke, especially where the front sprocket, swingarm pivot and rear axle approach a straight line. Use the bike manufacturer’s slack-check procedure. This viewer shows static placement. Illustrated chain-slack guide

    How the estimates work

    Current is parallel count times the cell’s continuous rating. Watts use nominal voltage. BMS, conductors, connectors, fusing and temperature may set a lower continuous pack limit. Weight uses maximum cell mass plus 2 lb for BMS, wire, copper, nickel and connectors. Case weight is extra.

    Cell model
    Compare straight and offset rows

    At equal center spacing, staggered rows use about 86.6% of the row height. That gives about 15.5% more cells per area in a large grid. The gain in your case depends on its edges, holders and clearances. Offset rows can make nickel or copper strips harder to cut.

    Installation, spacing and materials

    Perimeter-frame Kawasaki, Suzuki, Honda and Yamaha builds often use a tall pack lowered from above. Many steel-frame KTM and Husqvarna builds allow side entry. Check the opening and removal path on the actual model.

    Offset rows nest each cell between two neighbors. Holders maintain spacing and speed assembly, but their walls and lips use space. FR4 stays flat. Fishpaper can follow offset rows. Measure your materials and leave room for both terminals.

    Plan the current paths

    Read the S number on either end of a cell. Inside S1, join the positive terminals together and the negative terminals together. Then bridge S1 positive to S2 negative. Repeat through the last group.

    Rectangular banks often allow straight strips. Around a frame tube or motor cutout, inspect both faces and each bank for gaps and crossovers.

    Nickel placement

    Use the route’s bank, row and cell numbers to mark the parallel strips and series bridges on each face. A link between banks may need a separate insulated lead. Have your builder verify strip material, cross-section, current capacity and insulation before welding.

    Protect the cell groups

    Keep cells secure against vibration, including cells in the same parallel group. Rubbing can wear through wraps. Between series groups, exposed conductors at different voltages can short, spark and start a fire.

    Fire risk · exposed groups can short

    Secure the cells and add electrical-grade insulation before building. Thin FR4 fiberglass laminate resists puncture. Electrical-grade fishpaper protects against abrasion. Reserve ordinary cardstock and file folders for the cardboard mock-up.

    Holders restrain cells. An insulating barrier separates conductive strips and series groups. Measure the actual materials and reserve their thickness in the layout.

    Each series group has the chosen number of cells in parallel. Plan access for a balance connection at each series junction and room for insulation over the connections. Choose a BMS compatible with your cell chemistry, series count and current requirements. Measure its housing, connector access, power cable bends and the routes for small balance leads before finalizing cell count.

    Controller library · coming soon. Use a custom block in the advanced layout to reserve space for yours.

    For a metal enclosure, plan a continuous insulating barrier and edge protection at cable exits. FR4, G10, fishpaper or a suitably rated ABS liner may be options. Check thickness, abrasion, temperature and electrical ratings for the build.
